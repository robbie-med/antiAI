Medicare 2027 Physician Fee Schedule Proposed Rule

Policies with Potential to Improve Health Care Affordability for Patients and Taxpayers

July 23, 2026
Kendall Strong
Maya Sandalow
Manali Neeck
Allison Kwon
Daisy Kim
Kamryn Perry
Allison Buffett
Lisa Harootunian
Zach Gaumer
Share:
  • Share on Bluesky
  • Share on Facebook
  • Share on X
  • Share on LinkedIn

The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) recently released the Calendar Year 2027 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule (MPFS) Proposed Rule (MPFS CMS-1848-P).

These policy proposals may augment broader efforts to make health care more affordable for Medicare patients and taxpayers.

Affordability is central to the BPC Health Policy Program’s policy agenda from 2026 to 2030, as we identify durable bipartisan policies that improve the affordability of health care for consumers, employers, and taxpayers.

Below we highlight opportunities for stakeholders and policymakers to evaluate and debate affordability-related policies proposed within the proposed rule, as well as analyzing their impact on affordability.

Payment Reform

Current Procedural Terminology Request for Information

Proposed Policy: CMS formally requests stakeholder feedback on five specific questions as a part of a request for information about how physician payment rates are set, including the influence of the Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) coding system and the American Medical Association.

BPC Commentary: The larger question posed by this RFI is which data will be used to establish the relative value of different physician service codes. This is a critically important question to the debate about health care affordability. Thoughtful alternatives to the existing system should consider patient and systemic affordability and balance the need for primary and specialty care.

Practice Expense (PE) Relative Value Unit (RVU) Methodology

Proposed Policy: CMS proposes to modify the PE RVU methodology to reduce reliance on outdated, specialty-level practice expense survey data by phasing out the Indirect Practice Cost Index (IPCI) over two years. To limit year-to-year volatility, it also proposes a PE stabilization adjustment that caps the annual change in a code’s PE RVU at 5 percent.

BPC Commentary: Shifting toward more current, objective data could help ensure the fee schedule better reflects how care is delivered today. These budget-neutral updates would shift payment among services and improve affordability by ensuring Medicare pays more accurately for services, reducing the risk of over- or underpayment.

Medicare Shared Savings Program (MSSP): Growing ACO Participation

Proposed Policy: CMS proposes several changes to the MSSP to grow and sustain value-based care participation. CMS also proposes replacing the current risk-based methodology for advance investment payments with a flat, per-beneficiary quarterly payment that incentivizes serving beneficiaries who are low-income, dually eligible, or living in rural areas.

BPC Commentary: With these policies, CMS continues to advance value-based care as a path to affordability, strengthening the financial incentives for ACOs to generate genuine savings and take on greater accountability for costs. In particular, the proposed flat, per-beneficiary quarterly payment appears to appropriately extend accountable care to patients in need of better-coordinated care and may lower spending.

These proposals build on years of bipartisan attention to Medicare Part B physician payment reform. BPC’s September 2025 report, Toward Value and Sustainability: Medicare Part B Physician Payment and Related Reforms, highlights reforms to strengthen alternative payment models.

MSSP: Encouraging Advance Care Planning

Proposed Policy: CMS proposes changes to the MSSP’s beneficiary assignment methodology, updating the definition of primary care services to align with PFS payment proposals. Beginning in 2027, CMS proposes to include Advance Care Planning codes among the services considered for assignment, alongside new codes for Screening, Brief Intervention, Referral to Treatment, and Vaccine Adverse Effects Management.

BPC Health Commentary: If finalized, we anticipate assigned ACOs will improve coordination on beneficiaries’ care. Advance care planning can support affordability by better aligning care with patients’ goals and reducing  intensive interventions late in life that can drive unwanted and avoidable federal and out-of-pocket spending. See BPC’s 2022 report, An Updated Policy Roadmap: Caring for Those with Complex Needs, for reform opportunities to encourage advance care planning.

Primary and Behavioral Health Care

What CMS Is Requesting: CMS seeks comment on how to reconsider primary care service valuation, including how to appropriately value technology- and AI-enabled primary care. CMS frames this solicitation as part of a broader effort to redesign primary care, signaling that it views enhanced upstream, preventive care as central to the fee schedule’s next phase of reform.

BPC Commentary: As detailed in BPC’s June 2025 report, Strengthening Primary Care: Medicare Physician Payment and Related Reforms, strengthening primary care valuation and supporting prospective primary care payment would improve affordability by shifting spending toward prevention and chronic disease management. Prospective payment would give primary care practices more predictable revenue and greater flexibility to invest in care coordination, behavioral health integration, and other services.

Proposed Policy: CMS proposes to increase work RVUs for the Psychiatric Collaborative Care Model and related behavioral health integration codes.

BPC Commentary: If finalized, this would meaningfully increase how Medicare values collaborative care, one of the most evidence-based models for integrating behavioral health into primary care. Better integration supports affordability over the long term by reducing downstream costs stemming from emergency visits or hospitalizations.

Remote Patient Monitoring

Proposed Policy: CMS proposes four changes to remote patient monitoring (RPM) and remote therapeutic monitoring (RTM): 1) lower payment rates for several codes, 2) ban third-party vendors from providing RPM/RTM services on a practice’s behalf, 3) set established patient requirements for RTM, and 4) require a separately billable initiating visit before starting RPM/RTM services. CMS also seeks public comment on consolidating RPM/RTM codes to reduce administrative burden.

BPC Commentary: CMS’s RPM/RTM proposals cut two ways on affordability. Lower payment rates would reduce Medicare spending, but thin margins could dampen adoption in under-resourced and rural practices. Many practices also contract with third-party vendors to flag abnormal readings and respond quickly, and losing these vendors could jeopardize their RPM/RTM programs.

BPC has recommended several guardrails to curb low-value billing, including targeting remote monitoring services to the patients who need it most and strengthening federal oversight. CMS could increase its use of code modifiers to distinguish different types of monitoring. See BPC’s 2025 explainer, Maximizing the Value of Remote Patient Monitoring.

Telehealth

Proposed Policy: CMS proposes to add several new billing codes to the telehealth list, including for advance care planning, shared medical appointments, pediatric speech therapy, and vaccine adverse-effect management. It also proposes new modifiers to track telehealth platform contract relationships and incident-to billing arrangements, which allow nonphysicians to bill under a supervising physician’s identification.

BPC Commentary: CMS’s proposed modifiers do not directly affect payment, but they will improve the agency’s ability to analyze utilization and spending patterns. As BPC has recommended previously, the proposed modifiers will help CMS identify which types of providers are delivering telehealth, through what arrangements, and at what volume. This added visibility would support affordability over time by helping CMS detect low-value or wasteful billing. See BPC’s report, Positioning Telehealth Policy to Ensure High-Quality, Cost-Effective Care.

Artificial Intelligence

Proposed Policy: CMS proposes two notable AI-related policies. First, renaming “Software-as-a-Service (SaaS)” codes to “Software as a Medical Service (SaMS),” in alignment with other proposed payment rules, and paying for SaMS analyses performed on laboratory tests under the PFS rather than under the Clinical Laboratory Fee Schedule. Second, CMS proposes a new incentive to reward clinicians’ responsible use of AI in clinical and operational workflows. To qualify, clinicians must maintain organizational policies governing how AI-enabled tools are evaluated, deployed, and monitored, or help create or pilot such tools. Examples include risk-stratification models, clinical decision support, and predictive analytics for chronic disease management.

BPC Commentary: The SaMS proposal largely standardizes how these tools are categorized. The agency continues to work towards a more comprehensive approach and seeks to reduce payment rate variation across similar technologies. These proposals signal agency support for integrating AI into clinical and administrative workflows and gives participating clinicians a path to higher Medicare payments. We advise CMS and stakeholders to track whether increased AI use translates into measurably better patient outcomes or long-term cost savings.

Conclusion

CMS is accepting comments on the proposed rule until September 14, 2026. For a similar analysis of affordability-related reforms proposed in the Medicare Hospital Outpatient Prospective Payment System (OPPS), see BPC’s July 2026 explainer, Medicare 2027 Hospital Outpatient Proposed Rule. Visit our website for more information on BPC’s Health Program work.
