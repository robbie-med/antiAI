# Ref 27 — Nong P, Neprash HT. "Unintended Consequences of Using Ambient Artificial Intelligence Scribes for Billing." JAMA Health Forum 2026;7(1):e255771

doi:10.1001/jamahealthforum.2025.5771 · PMID 41511793 · Open access (CC-BY)
https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama-health-forum/fullarticle/2843723

Authors: Paige Nong, PhD & Hannah T. Neprash, PhD — University of Minnesota School of Public Health.
Published (PubMed issue date): 2026 Jan 2 — same issue as the Holmgren study (ref 21).

## Key verified content

- Ambient scribes cost **$300–$500 per month per clinician**.
- Vendors are "expanding beyond transcription and summarization to focus on billing."
- **Three unintended consequences:** (1) likely increases health care spending;
  (2) productivity pressure / upcoding may offset well-being gains; (3) erodes patient trust.

### Spending pathways (the mechanism)
| Pathway | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Higher-intensity billing | convert preventive → problem-based visits; code level 5 E/M instead of level 3 |
| Maximized diagnoses | more/severe diagnoses → higher value-based & Medicare Advantage risk-adjusted payments |
| Care-gap identification | flag overdue services that generate revenue if delivered in-system |

> "Simply justifying a more lucrative service code for an otherwise-identical office visit represents additional spending with no benefit to the patient."

### The recoupment math (this is the "check coming due")
> "A $500/month ambient scribe payment can be recouped in roughly 4 additional level-4 established patient Medicare office visits per month."

### Policy recommendations
1. National registry of AI use in care delivery organizations (does not yet exist).
2. Strengthen Medicare claims auditing to keep a clinician "in the loop" for all codes.
3. Automatic downward reimbursement adjustment for scribe-susceptible services (mirrors the
   existing **5.9%** Medicare Advantage coding-intensity adjustment).

Note: the essay's blockquote ("organizations may pressure clinicians to increase visits to
recoup costs") is from Neprash's NIHCM interview, linked from fn27 — the $300–500 figure and
the recoupment mechanism are in this article.
